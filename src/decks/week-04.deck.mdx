# The rest

SLOP3268 --- Week 4

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## A rest is not nothing

It is silence, written down.

It is timed as carefully as any note.

---

## Silence has a length

On a page of music, a **rest** is a mark that means "be quiet, for exactly
this long".

It is not the music stopping. It is part of the music. The score counts it
the same way it counts a pitch.

## The limit case

John Cage wrote a piece called 4'33". For four minutes and thirty-three
seconds, the player plays nothing on purpose.

So what do you hear? Whatever the room does. A cough. A chair. The air
vent. The piece becomes the sound that was already there.

---

## The cost and the gain

Cage gives up every note. That is a lot to give up.

In return, he makes you hear the room you were sitting in the whole time.
The cut is the whole idea.

---

## This week's exercise

Write thirty seconds of music with more rest than sound. Then, in one
paragraph, defend where each rest falls.

"I ran out of ideas" and "the rest is where you notice what you just
heard" are different answers. Only one is a cut that earned its place.

## Silence you can point to

A good rest is not empty time. It is a place you chose. You should be able
to say why it is there.

A pause in a song, a held breath in a film. Bring it.
