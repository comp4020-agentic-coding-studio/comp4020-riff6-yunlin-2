# Blank silk

SLOP3268 --- Week 3

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## Most of the painting is nothing

A few trees. A far bank. No people.

The rest is bare silk. And the bare silk is the point.

---

## One painter, taken to the edge

Ni Zan painted riverbanks in the Yuan dynasty.

- a small cluster of trees
- a low strip of far shore
- a wide, empty middle

He kept cutting until almost nothing was left. Then he stopped there.

---

## A word for the empty part

Chinese painters call it **liubai**. It means "leaving white".

It is not a way to save ink. The blank is doing real work in the picture.
It has a job, the same as any brushstroke.

The blank reads as water, as mist, and as distance at the same time. It
can hold all three because the painter never made it pick one.

---

## Why not just paint the water?

Paint the water, and it stops being the mist. Paint the mist, and it stops
being the distance.

Leaving it blank costs you a clear picture of any one thing. It buys you
all three at once.

---

## The same shape as last week

Last week the word for God was left blank on purpose. This week the silk
is left blank on purpose. Both say the empty space is more true, not just
more modest.

The lecture asks: is that one idea showing up twice, or two ideas that
only look alike?

## The gap is not a mistake

An unpainted middle is a choice. It carries the picture. Read it as work,
not as a part the painter forgot.
