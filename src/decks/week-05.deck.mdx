# The iceberg theory

SLOP3268 --- Week 5

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## Leave most of it out

Show a little on the page.

Cut the rest. The reader still feels it.

---

## Hemingway's claim

Hemingway cut the real ending of his story "Out of Season". The ending was
a suicide. He took it out completely.

His reason: if the writer truly knows the missing part, the reader feels
it anyway, even when it is never said.

The tip above the water is the small part you read. The large mass below
is everything the writer cut. It is unseen, but it holds the tip up.

---

## Does it actually work?

Maybe the reader really does feel the missing part. Or maybe the writer
just made a nice excuse after the fact.

That is the kind of question this course keeps hitting. This week we argue
it head on, instead of taking theology's or painting's word for it.

---

## A harder case: Carver and Lish

Raymond Carver wrote short stories. His editor, Gordon Lish, cut some by
more than half. The cut versions are the famous ones.

Here the author did not choose the cut. The editor did. And the story got
better by a rule neither of them had agreed on first.

---

## What would count as proof?

We want evidence that the reader feels what is missing.

A vague good feeling is not enough. We will look for what would show the
cut worked, and what would just be wishful thinking.
