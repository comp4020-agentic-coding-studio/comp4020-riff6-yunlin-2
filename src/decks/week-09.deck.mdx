# The null result

SLOP3268 --- Week 9

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## A finding, and a non-finding

You test an idea. It works. That is a positive result.

You test an idea. It doesn't. That is a null result.

Both tell you something true.

---

## Only one of them gets printed

A null result is just as informative as a positive one.

But journals and researchers both want a story with a finding in it. So the null result stays in the drawer.

Over time, the printed record fills up with wins and hides the misses.

The nulls stay hidden, so what gets read is a biased sample.

---

## Why the bias hurts

Say ten labs test the same weak idea. One gets a fluke positive by chance.

That one gets published. The nine nulls do not.

A reader sees the single win and thinks the idea is solid. It isn't.

---

## Trying to fix it

Some fixes push the null result back into the record.

- **Preregistration:** you say what you will test before you run it.
- **Registered reports:** a journal agrees to publish before it sees the result, win or not.
- **Null archives:** places built to keep the misses.

## Not a proud cut

Every other week, leaving something out was a choice someone stood behind.

This week it is closer to a failure the field is still trying to fix. What's missing here has a cost, and mostly nobody is paying it.
