# The invisible cut

SLOP3268 --- Week 6

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## The best cut is the one you never notice

Most films hide their cuts.

The join does its work, and you look right past it.

---

## How the hiding is done

Classical continuity editing is a set of small rules:

- the 180-degree rule (keep the camera on one side)
- shot then reverse shot (I speak, you speak)
- match on action (a move begun in one shot finishes in the next)

Each rule keeps the story smooth. Each rule hides the cut inside it.

---

## The opposite choice

Soviet montage does the reverse on purpose.

Eisenstein wanted the clash between two shots to *be* the meaning, not
something to smooth over.

So one tradition hides the join. The other puts the join on show.

The face never changes. The feeling comes from the join, shown here in amber.

---

## What this proves

The same shot of a still face reads as hunger, grief or desire.

The only thing that changed was the shot next to it.

So a cut can make meaning that neither shot held alone. That is a bolder
claim than painting or theology has made so far in this course.

## The cut is not empty

We think of a cut as a gap, a nothing between two pictures.

It is doing more work than either picture.
