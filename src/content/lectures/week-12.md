For eleven weeks we found the same move in theology, painting, music,
fiction, film, architecture, design, statistics, mathematics and
politics. That pattern is itself suspicious. A course that argues
restraint is good practice should notice when it keeps picking the
examples that fit. This lecture is co-taught on purpose, so the two of us
can disagree about it on the record.

Two counter-cases sit on the table. First, maximalist art that wins by
refusing to leave anything out, like a Kehinde Wiley portrait or a
Bollywood musical number. Second, the plain fact that most bad, lazy work
is also missing things, whether that is an unfinished essay, a thin
argument, or a rushed edit. If leaving something out were enough for
quality, both of those would be good work. They are not. So the
semester's thesis has always needed a second clause it mostly left
unsaid. Not every cut earns its keep. This course has been better at
showing you cuts that worked than at teaching you to tell the difference
in advance. That second clause is the final assessment's real subject.

## Outline

- maximalism as the semester's most direct counter-example
- why "it left something out" can't be enough on its own, since bad work
  does too
- the second, mostly unstated clause the whole course has been assuming
