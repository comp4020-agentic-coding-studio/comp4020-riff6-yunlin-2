A redacted government document is the most literal object in this
course. Black bars sit over specific words. The omission is printed and
handed to you. But the more interesting cases are the ones where nothing
looks missing at all. An economic model can leave a cost out of its sums,
whether that cost is carbon, care work, or noise. It isn't hiding
anything. It is just declining to count it, and the numbers still add up.
A political speech can name only the opponent's plan and never its own.
It persuades by omission while looking like a complete argument.

This week is the direct rehearsal for the final assessment. An argument
whose force depends on what it refuses to say is a redaction with the
black bars taken off. It is the same move as everything in weeks 2
through 10. The stakes are just higher, because now the fight is over who
gets to decide what counts as background.
