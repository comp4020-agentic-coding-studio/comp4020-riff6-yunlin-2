Classical continuity editing is a set of techniques for hiding the cut
inside the story rather than showing it. The 180-degree rule, shot and
reverse shot, and the match on action all keep the join out of sight. It
is the most successful disappearing act in this course. Most people
watching a conventionally edited film could not tell you where a single
cut fell, because the whole system is built to make that question feel
beside the point. The smoothness costs you the join. It buys you a story
you never have to look away from.

The Soviet montage tradition does the opposite on purpose. Eisenstein
wanted the clash between two shots to be the meaning, not something to
smooth over. The lecture uses the Kuleshov effect to show why this
matters. Take the same shot of an actor's still, neutral face. Cut it
next to a bowl of soup and it reads as hunger. Cut it next to a coffin
and it reads as grief. Cut it next to a person and it reads as desire.
The face never changes. Only the shot beside it changes. So a cut can
make meaning that neither shot held alone, which is a bolder claim than
painting or theology has made so far.
