A reductio does not prove its conclusion by building toward it. It proves
by ruling out the alternative until only one option can still be standing.
Mathematicians talk about an "elegant" proof the way a film editor talks
about a clean cut, meaning fewer moves and nothing carried that is not
load-bearing. Occam's razor is the same instinct made general. Prefer the
account that needs the fewest assumptions, not because simpler is prettier,
but because every extra assumption is one more thing that could be wrong.

Paul Erdős imagined a book God keeps, holding the most elegant proof of
every theorem. He called it "The Book". The joke makes plain what this
course has been circling since week 1. A discipline can share a quiet,
largely unspoken standard for which cut was the right one. That standard
is argued over, sometimes overturned, and taken completely seriously by
people who would never call it taste.

## Outline

- reductio ad absurdum: proving by elimination rather than construction
- Occam's razor as elimination promoted to a general principle
- Erdős's Book, and mathematics's own version of "taste is what you
  leave out"
