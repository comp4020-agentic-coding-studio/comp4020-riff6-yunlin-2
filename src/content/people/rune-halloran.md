## The crime

Rune could not bear an empty space, or an unattributed one. They padded thin
manuscripts into fat ones, wrote over other people's silences with borrowed
prose, and passed off, as a single dreadful habit, both the plagiarism of what
others had written and the over-writing of what should have stayed blank. Their
crime was addition mistaken for authorship: the compulsion to fill every rest
with a note, preferably someone else's.

## The sentence

The pattern was caught by a scholar who recognised her own deleted paragraph
still living inside Rune's. They served two years. They report that the hardest
discipline of the cell was its long, unfillable quiet, which no amount of talking
would make go away.

## What the course subtracted

Week 3, "Blank silk," showed them that the untouched part of a scroll is not a
lack but a made decision, the most confident stroke in the picture. Week 4, "The
rest," taught them the same lesson in time: that an earned silence carries the
piece, and that a note you choose not to play is still yours to have chosen. They
write very little now, and sign only the little they write. The blank, they say,
was theirs all along; they had simply been too frightened to leave it there.
