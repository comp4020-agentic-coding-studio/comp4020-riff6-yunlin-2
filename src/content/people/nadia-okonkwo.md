## The crime

Nadia was a chief financial officer with a gift for the waterline. Above it, her
reports were immaculate: growth, margin, a company in perfect health. Below it,
out of sight of any quarterly summary, sat an iceberg of losses she moved from
column to column so that no single page ever showed the whole mass. She did not
forge a number so much as choose which nine tenths of the truth to submerge.

## The sentence

Icebergs are patient, and hers surfaced at last into an audit that could not be
steered around. She served four years. She spent them, by her own account,
learning to be honest about the part of a thing that does not show.

## What the course subtracted

Two weeks did the work. Week 5, "The iceberg theory," taught her that what sits
beneath the surface is still hers to account for, that omitting the submerged
mass is a lie in the shape of a fact. Week 9, "The null result," taught her the
harder courage of publishing the thing that failed, the loss that returned
nothing, the report nobody wants filed. She audits for a living now, and looks
first, always, at whatever a statement has arranged for you not to see.
