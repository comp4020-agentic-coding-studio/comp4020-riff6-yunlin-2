## The crime

Petra ran a self-storage empire and a private catastrophe in the same buildings.
She could not throw anything out, and she discovered that a person who keeps
everything can hide anything: receipts she owed to creditors, inventories she
owed to auditors, whole liabilities filed away under boxes of genuinely
worthless things. Her fraud was not a shredder but its opposite; she buried the
evidence by refusing to remove a single sheet of it.

## The sentence

It took a bankruptcy trustee three months and a skip the size of a small barge
to recover what she had concealed by keeping. She served eighteen months. She
has said the cell was the first room she had ever been unable to fill.

## What the course subtracted

Week 8, "Less is a verb," undid the belief that had ruined her: that subtraction
is a state you drift into once the keeping stops, rather than an action you have
to perform, deliberately, against your own grip. She learned to throw the first
thing out on purpose, and then the second. She keeps a nearly empty flat now,
and says the emptiness is the only thing she has ever owned outright.
