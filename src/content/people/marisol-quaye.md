## The crime

Marisol built a small, exquisite fraud out of things that were not there. She
forged manuscripts of a negative theology that no library had ever held, aged
the vellum, and let scholars fill the blanks with their own longing. Her
masterpiece was a series of near-blank canvases sold as lost minimalist
originals; she had learned that a buyer will pay most for the part of a picture
the artist supposedly withheld. She sold absence as provenance, and for a long
time absence did not object.

## The sentence

The forgeries came apart when a curator asked, politely, to inspect the thing
that was missing. She served four years. She spent the first of them furious at
the flatness of a cell and the last of them grateful for it, which is a longer
sentence than any court hands down.

## What the course subtracted

In the prison library she found the real apophatic tradition, the one she had
only ever counterfeited: the discipline of describing what language cannot reach
by refusing to say the wrong thing about it. Via negativa taught her the
difference between a blank that means something and a blank that merely hides a
lie. She stopped fabricating what a work was not there to say, and founded the
course so that others could learn to subtract honestly. She still signs nothing
she did not make.
