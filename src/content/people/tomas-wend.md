## The crime

Tomas never once told a provable lie, and boasted of it. His art was the
implication: the pause that let a mark conclude he was a doctor, the letterhead
that suggested a bank without naming one, the testimony technically true in
every word and false in its whole. He won by what he left unsaid, and for years
he believed that silence could not be perjury, because silence signs nothing.

## The sentence

A judge disagreed, at length, about the distance between what he said and what
he meant a jury to hear. He served three years for the arguments he had made by
making none. He thought the verdict unfair right up until the morning he finally
understood it.

## What the course subtracted

Week 11, "The unsaid argument," is where the reckoning caught up with the
vocabulary for it. The course taught him the ethics of omission: that you are
accountable for what you leave out, that an implication is a claim you have
merely declined to sign, and that the honest omission and the dishonest one look
identical until you ask what each was for. He talks a good deal less now, and
means all of it.
